# Post-Harvest Guide — Muscat Italia (Italia) Grapes

> **Key principle:** Muscat Italia is a **non-climacteric** variety — ripening stops the moment grapes are detached from the vine. Harvest timing is therefore irreversible. Every subsequent step in the post-harvest chain exists to preserve the quality achieved on the vine.

---

## 1. Harvest & On-Vine Storage

### Maturity Indicators
Harvest is triggered by a combination of:
- Berry size and **golden-yellow colour**
- Berry **firmness** and skin integrity
- **Sugar level: 16–20° Brix**
- Characteristic muscat aroma development

### Late Forcing (Forzatura tardiva)
A technique common in southern Italy: grape canopies are covered with plastic film in August, delaying harvest from October through to December. This **on-vine storage** keeps stalks hydrated and green, allowing producers to supply premium markets — notably the Christmas season — without cold room infrastructure.

---

## 2. Precooling & Initial Treatments

**Rapid pre-cooling** is the single most urgent step after harvest. Freshly harvested grapes must undergo rapid pre-cooling to slow down respiration and metabolic activities — every hour at ambient temperature accelerates deterioration and shortens shelf life irreversibly.

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Temperature reduction** | Must happen rapidly after harvest to slow respiration and metabolic activity |
| **Target temperature** | Bring to 0–2°C within 4–6 hours of picking |
| **SO₂ fumigation** | Initial fumigation during pre-cooling to control *Botrytis cinerea* (grey mould) |
| **SO₂ generator pads** | Placed inside packaging boxes for continuous protection during transport |

Standard commercial practice involves **initial SO₂ fumigation during pre-cooling**, often followed by the use of **SO₂ generator pads placed inside packaging boxes** for the duration of transport. Both steps together form the industry-standard SO₂ protocol.

> ⚠️ Never leave freshly harvested grapes at ambient temperature for more than 2 hours. Field heat is the primary cause of post-harvest quality loss.

---

## 3. Specialised Storage Techniques

### Cold Storage

**Optimal Temperature:** Grapes should be stored in cold rooms at **-1 to 0°C**. They age rapidly if exposed to temperatures above 20°C — even brief exposure during loading or transport will accelerate deterioration significantly.

**High Humidity:** A relative humidity (RH) of **95%** is essential to keep the stalks (rachis) hydrated and green while preventing the berries from wilting. Humidity below 90% causes rapid stalk browning and berry shrivelling.

- **Target temperature:** -1 to 0°C
- **Relative humidity:** 95%
- **Maximum duration:** ~4 weeks at target quality

### Modified Atmosphere Packaging (MAP)
Sealing grapes in polypropylene trays with an active atmosphere (e.g. **20% CO₂**) delivers:
- Significantly reduced cluster deterioration rate
- Delayed chlorophyll degradation in stalks (keeps them green)
- Minimised weight loss

### ⚠️ The "Fizzy Taste" Risk
Extended MAP storage beyond **21 days** at high CO₂ concentrations can trigger anaerobic fermentation, leading to accumulation of **ethanol and ethyl acetate** — resulting in an undesirable fizzy or fermented taste. Monitor duration carefully.

---

## 4. Cleaning, Sorting & Packaging

### Sorting Protocol
Before storage, grapes must be sorted to **remove all diseased, damaged, or deformed berries** to prevent the spread of infection. A single infected berry can spread *Botrytis* to adjacent fruit within 24–48 hours in cold storage.
- Sort into grades: **Extra / Class I / Class II**
- Perform sorting in a cool, shaded area — never in direct sun
- Use clean scissors; never pull berries by hand

### Packaging
Grapes should be kept in **sealed breathable bags or perforated plastic containers** to balance moisture retention with airflow. This prevents moisture buildup on berry surfaces (which promotes mould) while maintaining the high ambient humidity needed to prevent wilting.

### Packaging Formats
| Format | Use case |
|---|---|
| Sealed breathable bag | Retail / supermarket shelf |
| Perforated plastic container | Wholesale, local market |
| 5 kg vented export carton + SO₂ pad | Export to Europe / Middle East |
| 4.5 kg cageot | Local Moroccan market |

> Never use airtight non-perforated packaging — trapped moisture accelerates Botrytis and causes berry breakdown.

## 5. Emerging Technologies

### Edible Coatings
Thin layers of edible materials — **pectin, aloe vera** — create a semi-permeable gas and water vapour barrier that:
- Reduces weight loss
- Maintains berry firmness
- Offers an alternative to traditional plastic packaging

Research is ongoing; commercial adoption is limited but growing.

### Ethylene Management
Although Muscat Italia produces very low ethylene levels, it is **sensitive** to ethylene exposure from other produce. Ethylene stimulates formation of the abscission layer, causing **berry detachment** (shattering).

Mitigation:
- Separate from ethylene-producing fruits (apples, pears, bananas)
- Maintain adequate cold room airflow
- Consider ethylene scrubbers in long-term storage

## 6. Transportation

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Transport resistance** | Good — but degrades rapidly above 20°C |
| **Target temperature in transit** | 0–2°C (refrigerated truck, ATP-certified) |
| **SO₂ pad** | Mandatory in each export carton |
| **Maximum transit time** | 48–72 hours (Tanger Med → Rungis by road) |
| **Cold chain breaks** | Zero tolerance — even 2–3 hours at ambient causes condensation and mould |

> The cold chain is the single most important factor in delivering grapes to the consumer in a freshly-harvested state. A perfect harvest ruined by a broken cold chain cannot be recovered.

## Quick Reference — Post-Harvest Timeline

```
Harvest (6–10am)
    ↓ immediately
Move to shade — sort & pack
    ↓ within 2 hours
Pre-cool to 0–2°C
    ↓
Cold room storage (-1 to 0°C, 95% RH)
    ↓ same day or next morning
Load refrigerated truck
    ↓ 48–72 hours
Delivery to buyer (Rungis / local / packer)
```

## Key Risks Summary

| Risk | Cause | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Berry softening | Field heat, broken cold chain | Pre-cool within 2h of harvest |
| *Botrytis* mould | Damaged berries, high humidity without SO₂ | SO₂ pads, careful sorting |
| Fizzy taste | MAP >21 days at high CO₂ | Monitor storage duration |
| Berry detachment | Ethylene exposure, rough handling | Separate from ethylene sources, gentle transport |
| Stalk browning | Low humidity, warm temperatures | 95% RH, 0°C storage |
